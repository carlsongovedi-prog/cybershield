# Small Business Security Checklist (Uganda)

**Business:** ____________  **Checked by:** ____________  **Date:** ________
Tick each box when done. Review every 3 months.

## 1. Money and mobile money (MTN MoMo, Airtel Money, bank apps)
- [ ] Never share your mobile money PIN, one-time codes or bank PIN with anyone, including "support", agents, suppliers or staff.
- [ ] Use a separate SIM and phone for the business wallet if possible, kept out of casual use.
- [ ] Lock the SIM with a SIM PIN and the phone with a screen lock. Record the SIM's registered owner details and keep them current.
- [ ] If the phone suddenly loses signal for no reason, call the provider on a different phone at once. It may be a SIM swap.
- [ ] Approve payments with two people for large amounts: one prepares, another approves.
- [ ] Confirm every new supplier's payment details by phone, using a number you already had. Never trust a "we changed our account" message.
- [ ] Check the wallet and bank statements weekly. Report unknown transactions the same day.
- [ ] Turn on transaction alerts. Keep the provider's official hotline saved in the phone.

## 2. WhatsApp and social accounts
- [ ] Turn on **two-step verification** in WhatsApp (Settings > Account > Two-step verification) and add a recovery email.
- [ ] Never share the 6-digit WhatsApp code that arrives by SMS. Anyone who has it can take over your number.
- [ ] Check **Linked devices** monthly and log out any device you don't recognise.
- [ ] Be careful with messages from "family", "the boss" or a "friend" asking for money or airtime. Call them before you pay.
- [ ] Don't open unknown links, APK files or "job" and "prize" offers. Don't scan QR codes you didn't ask for.
- [ ] Set who can add you to groups to **My contacts**. Turn off auto-download of media in untrusted chats.
- [ ] Use separate business and personal numbers. Keep admin rights on business groups with at least two trusted people.
- [ ] Turn on two-step verification for Facebook, Instagram and TikTok business pages too.

## 3. Accounts and passwords
- [ ] Use a password manager. One unique password per account, at least 12 characters.
- [ ] Turn on **MFA** for email, banking, cloud storage and social media. Prefer an authenticator app over SMS.
- [ ] Protect the main business email above everything else. It can reset all your other accounts.
- [ ] Give each person their own login. No shared passwords or shared admin accounts.
- [ ] When a staff member leaves, remove access the same day (email, WhatsApp groups, wallets, shared drives, Wi-Fi).

## 4. Computers and phones
- [ ] Turn on automatic updates for the operating system and apps. Don't use unlicensed or cracked software.
- [ ] Turn on full-disk encryption (BitLocker, FileVault) and a screen lock.
- [ ] Use a standard account for daily work. Keep the admin account for installing software only.
- [ ] Install apps only from official stores. Remove apps you don't use.
- [ ] Use reputable security software and run a scan after any suspicious event.
- [ ] Use only trusted USB drives and scan them before opening files.

## 5. Network and Wi-Fi
- [ ] Change the router's default admin password and Wi-Fi name. Use WPA2 or WPA3 with a strong passphrase.
- [ ] Put customers on a **guest Wi-Fi**, separate from staff and the point-of-sale or office computers.
- [ ] Turn off remote router administration and WPS if you don't use them.
- [ ] Avoid doing banking or wallet work on public Wi-Fi. Use mobile data instead.

## 6. Backups and recovery (3-2-1)
- [ ] Keep 3 copies of important data (sales records, customer list, accounts), on 2 kinds of storage, with 1 copy offsite or in trusted cloud storage.
- [ ] Back up weekly. **Test a restore** every quarter.
- [ ] Keep an offline copy so ransomware can't reach it.
- [ ] Keep paper copies of key contacts: bank, mobile money provider, internet provider, IT helper.

## 7. Customer data and staff awareness
- [ ] Collect only the customer data you need. Keep it locked and share it only with those who need it. (Uganda's Data Protection and Privacy Act, 2019, requires reasonable safeguards for personal data.)
- [ ] Don't keep customer IDs or photos in open WhatsApp groups or personal phones.
- [ ] Teach staff the three phishing signs: urgency, a strange sender, and a request for a PIN, password or link click.
- [ ] Agree who to tell, and how fast, if someone clicks something suspicious or a wallet looks wrong.

## 8. If something goes wrong
1. Stop. Don't reply or click again.
2. Disconnect the affected device from the internet.
3. Change passwords and PINs from a **clean** device. Log out all sessions.
4. Call the mobile money provider or bank hotline to freeze or block.
5. Tell the owner or manager. Write down what happened and when.
6. Restore from backup if needed, and review this checklist.
